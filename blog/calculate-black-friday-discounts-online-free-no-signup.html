How to Calculate Black Friday Discounts Online

Check the deal before you pay — final price, savings and the real discount %, in seconds.

November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

To calculate a Black Friday discount, open the free Discount Calculator, type the original price and the discount %, and read the final price and your savings straight away. Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday 27 November, and most stores run sale prices for several days around it — so a quick check on every deal is worth ten seconds. It is free, needs no sign-up, and your prices never leave your browser.

What the Discount Calculator does

The Discount Calculator has two tabs, and each answers a different shopping question. The first tab, Discount → sale price, is for a tag that says “40% off”: type the original price and the discount %, and it shows the final price, how much you save, and the discount applied. The second tab, Sale price → discount %, works the other way round: type the original price and the sale price, and it tells you the real discount % — the tool for checking a “was / now” label.

Both tabs update live as you type; there is also a Calculate button and a Reset button that clears all four fields. Results show with two decimals and comma separators, so 2999.4 reads as 2,999.40. The tool shows amounts with a $ sign — the maths is identical in every currency, so just read the numbers as your own: type rupees in, and 2,999.40 means Rs 2,999.40. Everything runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Calculate a Black Friday discount in 5 steps

  1. Open the Discount Calculator. It starts on the “Discount → sale price” tab, the one you need for a “% off” deal.
  2. Type the original price — the price before the discount, exactly as shown on the tag. It must be greater than zero.
  3. Type the discount %. The tool only accepts 0 to 100 — a discount above 100% is not a discount, it is the shop paying you.
  4. Read the answer as you type. The final price appears in large type, with “You save” and “Discount applied” underneath.
  5. For a “was / now” deal, switch tabs. Click “Sale price → discount %”, type the was-price and the now-price, and the tool shows the real discount %, what you save and what you pay.

Worked example 1: a jacket at 40% off

A jacket is marked 4,999 with 40% off. Type 4999 and 40 into the first tab. The discount is 4,999 × 40 ÷ 100 = 1,999.60, so the final price is 4,999 − 1,999.60 = 2,999.40. The tool shows exactly that: Final price 2,999.40, You save 1,999.60, Discount applied 40%.

Worked example 2: is “was 7,499, now 5,249” really 30% off?

Headphones are labelled was 7,499, now 5,249, and the sign claims 30% off. Switch to the “Sale price → discount %” tab and type both prices. You save 7,499 − 5,249 = 2,250. The discount is 2,250 ÷ 7,499 × 100 = 30.00%, so the claim checks out this time: Discount 30%, You save 2,250, You pay 5,249. The same tab catches labels that do not check out — a quietly raised was-price shows up as a lower real %.

Worked example 3: stacked discounts do not add up

A TV costs 12,000. The store offers 50% off, then a further 20% off the reduced price at checkout. It is tempting to add 50 + 20 and call it 70% off — it is not. After the first cut the TV is 12,000 × 50% = 6,000. The second cut takes 20% off that 6,000, which is 1,200, leaving 6,000 − 1,200 = 4,800. Your total saving is 12,000 − 4,800 = 7,200, which is 7,200 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = 60% off — a full 1,200 more than the 3,600 the “70% off” guess would have promised. Stacked discounts multiply: you pay 50% of 80%, which is 40% of the original price.

6 mistakes that make a Black Friday deal look better than it is

  • Adding stacked discounts. 50% off plus a further 20% off is 60% off in total, not 70% — the second cut applies to the already-reduced price.
  • Trusting “up to 70% off”. The biggest % usually applies to a handful of items. Check the item you actually want with the reverse tab instead of assuming the headline % applies to it.
  • Accepting an inflated was-price. A was-price that was never the real selling price makes any discount look big. Compare it with the same item's price a few weeks ago, or at another store.
  • Forgetting delivery and tax. A 2,999.40 jacket is not a 2,999.40 purchase once delivery is added — check the final price at checkout, not just the discount.
  • Typing the prices in the wrong boxes. In the reverse tab, the original price goes first and the sale price second. Swap them and the tool tells you the sale price is higher than the original — “that's a markup, not a discount”.
  • Rounding in your head. 33% off 2,999 is not “about 1,000 off”. It is 2,999 × 33 ÷ 100 = 989.67, so you pay 2,009.33. Small rounding slips add up across a basket of sale items.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Discount Calculator free to use?
Yes. Completely free, no sign-up, no app — it runs entirely in your browser, and the prices you type never leave your device.

Does the Discount Calculator work for rupees, pounds or euros?
Yes. The maths is the same in every currency. The tool shows amounts with a $ sign and two decimals — just read the numbers as your own currency, so a result of 2,999.40 means Rs 2,999.40 if you typed rupees in.

How do I check whether a Black Friday “was” price is real?
Use the “Sale price → discount %” tab: type the was-price and the sale price and it shows the real discount %. Then compare the was-price with what the same item cost a few weeks ago, or at another store. If the was-price was never the real selling price, the discount % is not real either.

Can I add two discounts together, like 50% + 20% = 70% off?
No. Stacked discounts multiply, they do not add. 50% off, then a further 20% off the reduced price, leaves you paying 50% of 80% — that is 40% of the original price, so 60% off in total, not 70%.
