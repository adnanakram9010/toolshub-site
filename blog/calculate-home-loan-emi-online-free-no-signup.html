A home loan is the biggest loan most people ever take, and the EMI decides whether you can afford it. On 23 November 2026 the Prime Minister set banks a target of disbursing Rs1 trillion in home loans over six months under the Apna Ghar Programme — up to Rs10 million over 20 years, at a fixed 5% markup for the first 10 years, with a 10% borrower contribution. What a home loan EMI actually is

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment: a fixed monthly payment covering part of the principal (the amount you borrowed) plus interest on the outstanding balance. You pay the same total every month, but the split changes — early payments are mostly interest, later payments mostly principal — and the balance reaches zero with the final payment. Almost all home loans use the reducing-balance method: interest is charged only on what you still owe, so every extra payment cuts the balance and the interest on it.

The standard EMI formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the number of monthly payments. Worked example: Rs 10,000,000 at 5% for 20 years

Maximum Apna Ghar loan: Rs 10,000,000 at 5% a year over 20 years (240 payments), monthly rate 5 ÷ 12, about 0.4167%:

  • Monthly EMI: 65,995.57
  • Total payable: 15,838,937.74 (EMI × 240 payments)
  • Total interest: 5,838,937.74 (total payable minus the 10,000,000 principal)

In the first year you pay 493,215.75 in interest and only 298,731.14 of principal, leaving a balance of 9,701,268.86 — of the 791,946.84 paid in year one, 62.3% is interest, because the balance is largest at the start.

With the 10% down payment: on a Rs 10,000,000 house you contribute Rs 1,000,000 and borrow Rs 9,000,000 — at 5% over 20 years the EMI is 59,396.02, total interest 5,255,043.97 and total payable 14,255,043.97.

One important detail: the 5% rate is fixed for the first 10 years only — a market rate applies after that. The calculator takes one rate for the whole term, so use it in two runs: 10 years at 5%, leaving a balance of 6,222,151.83, then enter that balance as the new loan amount with the new rate and the remaining 10 years.

How the loan term changes your EMI (same loan: Rs 10,000,000 at 5%)

  • 10 years: EMI 106,065.52 — total interest 2,727,861.83.
  • 20 years: EMI 65,995.57 — total interest 5,838,937.74 (the Apna Ghar maximum term).
  • 30 years: EMI 53,682.16 — total interest 9,325,578.43. That is 6,597,716.60 more interest than the 10-year option.

That is the central trade-off: a longer term shrinks the monthly payment but stretches the interest over more years, so the total cost rises steeply. Pick the shortest term you can pay comfortably every month.

Why the interest rate matters more than the loan size

Take a smaller loan at a market rate: Rs 5,000,000 at 12% over 20 years gives an EMI of 55,054.31 and total interest of 8,213,033.60 — 2,374,095.86 more than the Rs 10,000,000 loan at 5%, though the loan is half the size. The rate, not the loan size, decides what a home loan really costs.

5 mistakes that make your home loan EMI wrong

  • Entering the house price instead of the loan amount. The EMI is calculated on what you borrow — on a Rs 10,000,000 house with 10% down, that is Rs 9,000,000.
  • Using one rate for the whole term when it changes. Under Apna Ghar the 5% rate lasts 10 years, then a market rate applies — 20 years at 5% understates what you will pay.
  • Mixing up monthly and annual rates. The formula needs the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12) — confirm whether the quoted rate is annual.
  • Comparing EMI instead of total cost. Two loans can have similar EMIs but very different total interest — compare total payable (EMI × number of payments), not just the monthly figure.
  • Budgeting to the last rupee. An EMI that leaves nothing for emergencies is fragile — one unexpected expense and you miss a payment. Keep a buffer.

Frequently asked questions

What is a home loan EMI?
EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed amount you pay every month towards a home loan, covering part of the principal and interest on the outstanding balance. The total stays the same, but the interest portion shrinks and the principal portion grows as the balance falls.

How do I calculate EMI for an Apna Ghar loan at 5%?
Type the loan amount, 5 as the annual rate and 20 as the term into the EMI / Loan Calculator and click Calculate EMI — for a Rs 10,000,000 loan the EMI is 65,995.57 a month. Remember the 5% rate is fixed for the first 10 years only; after that a market rate applies, so re-run the remaining balance at the new rate.

Does a longer home loan term mean a lower EMI?
Yes — spreading the same loan over more years lowers each payment, but you pay interest for longer, so the total interest rises steeply. On a Rs 10,000,000 loan at 5%, going from 10 to 30 years cuts the EMI from 106,065.52 to 53,682.16 but raises total interest from 2,727,861.83 to 9,325,578.43.

What is the 10% down payment on a home loan?
Under Apna Ghar the borrower contributes 10% of the house price and borrows the other 90%. On a Rs 10,000,000 house that is Rs 1,000,000 of your own savings and a loan of Rs 9,000,000 — at 5% over 20 years, an EMI of 59,396.02.
