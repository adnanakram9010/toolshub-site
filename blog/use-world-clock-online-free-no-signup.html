How to Use a World Clock Online

See what time it is anywhere in the world at a glance — live, every second, with no signup.

November 17, 2026 · 6 min read

A world clock shows the current time in several cities at once, so you can see at a glance what time it is in London, New York, Tokyo and more. ToolsHub's World Clock shows 12 major cities on one screen, updates every second, and lets you add 19 more — free, with no signup, and nothing to install.

That one screen replaces the mental maths most of us get wrong: adding or subtracting hours in our head, forgetting daylight saving, and missing that it can be a different day elsewhere. Whether you are calling family abroad or joining a remote team meeting, the answer is in front of you in a second.

What the World Clock shows

Open the World Clock and the grid already shows 12 major cities: New York, London, Paris, Berlin, Dubai, Karachi, Mumbai, Singapore, Tokyo, Sydney, Los Angeles and São Paulo. Each card shows the city name, the time, and the date there — for example, "Mon, 16 Nov 2026" under the time.

Times are in 24-hour format with seconds, such as 14:05:32, and every clock ticks live. The date matters too: late evening in Los Angeles can already be the next day in Karachi, and the date line under each clock catches that. A dropdown below the grid offers 19 more cities: Chicago, Toronto, Vancouver, Anchorage, Honolulu, Mexico City, Buenos Aires, Reykjavik, Moscow, Istanbul, Cairo, Johannesburg, Riyadh, Bangkok, Jakarta, Manila, Hong Kong, Seoul and Auckland. Everything is calculated in your browser, so nothing is sent to any server.

Use the World Clock in 4 steps

  1. Open the World Clock. The 12-city grid is already there — no setup, no account. The times start ticking the moment the page loads.
  2. Read the time and the date together. The large number is the time in 24-hour format: 23:00 means 11:00 PM and 02:00 means 2:00 AM. The smaller line underneath is the date there — check it, because the day can differ across the dateline.
  3. Add a city if you need it. Pick a city from the dropdown under the grid and press "Add city" — it appears as a new card with a "✕ Remove" button. If the city is already on the clock, the tool warns you instead. Once all 19 are added, the dropdown reads "All cities added" and the button is disabled.
  4. Remove a city you added. Press "✕ Remove" on any card you added and it disappears, returning the city to the dropdown. The original 12 cities have no remove button; they are always there.

Planning a meeting across time zones

Take London, Karachi and Singapore. A 10:00 call in London is 15:00 in Karachi and 18:00 in Singapore — a working hour for London and Karachi, just after hours in Singapore. The kindest window is London's morning: 09:00–12:00 in London is 14:00–17:00 in Karachi and 17:00–20:00 in Singapore.

Karachi and New York are harder: no hour suits a 9-to-5 day for both. 09:00 in Karachi is 23:00 the previous night in New York, and 17:00 in New York is 03:00 the next morning in Karachi. The only overlap is New York's morning against Karachi's evening — 09:00 in New York is 19:00 in Karachi — so one side always takes the evening.

Why the times change through the year: daylight saving

Many countries move their clocks forward in summer and back in winter, on different dates — so the gap between two cities changes during the year. In 2026 the United States put its clocks back on 1 November and the United Kingdom on 25 October, while Sydney started daylight time on 4 October and Auckland on 27 September. New York is 10 hours behind Karachi in November (UTC−5 against UTC+5), but only 9 hours behind in summer (UTC−4). London is GMT (UTC+0) in November and British Summer Time (UTC+1) in summer; Sydney is UTC+11 in November and UTC+10 in winter.

You do not need to track any of this: the World Clock uses your browser's own time-zone data, so when a city moves its clocks, the time shown follows automatically. Some places never change at all: São Paulo has had no daylight saving since 2019, and Dubai, Karachi, Mumbai, Singapore and Tokyo never move their clocks.

5 mistakes that make world-clock maths go wrong

  • Reading 24-hour time as 12-hour time. The tool shows 23:00, not 11:00 PM; 02:00 is 2:00 AM, 12:00 is noon and 00:00 is midnight. A quick misread turns a morning call into a midnight one.
  • Forgetting the date changes. When it is 23:00 on 15 November in Los Angeles, it is already 16 November in Karachi — always read the date line, not just the time.
  • Assuming the offset never changes. New York is 10 hours behind Karachi in November but 9 hours behind in summer — re-check the gap after the clocks change, or just look at the live clock.
  • Ignoring half-hour zones. Mumbai is 30 minutes ahead of Karachi — a "same time" guess is wrong by half an hour, which is enough to miss a call.
  • Booking by your clock only. A 09:00 meeting that suits you can be 02:00 or 04:00 for the other side — convert the time for every attendee first.
