How to Use a Coin Flip & Dice Roller Online

Settle a decision or roll for your game in about a second — fair, free, and no signup.

November 7, 2026 · 6 min read

Open the Coin Flip & Dice Roller, pick the Coin flip or Dice roller tab, and click Flip coin or Roll dice — the result appears in about a second, with a running heads/tails tally or the dice total shown beside the faces. It is free, needs no signup, and everything runs in your browser.

A coin flip is the simplest fair decision-maker there is: two outcomes, no argument. Dice add just enough range for board games, classroom probability, and picking between several options. The practical problem is the props — no coin in your pocket, no dice on the table. An online roller removes them entirely, and this one is built to be genuinely fair rather than just looking fair.

What the Coin Flip & Dice Roller does

The tool has two tabs. The Coin flip tab shows a large coin face — a question mark before the first flip — with Flip coin and Reset tally buttons. Each flip takes about a second: the coin spins, then lands and announces the result, for example "It's Heads!". Below it, a tally keeps a running count of Heads, Tails and Total flips.

The Dice roller tab has a Number of dice dropdown from 1 to 6, set to 2 by default, and a Roll dice button. The dice tumble for under a second, then settle as pip faces with the total underneath, for example "Total: 7". Both tabs run entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Fairness is where this tool earns its keep. Results come from your browser's cryptographically secure random generator (crypto.getRandomValues), not a quick formula. For dice, out-of-range values are discarded before the remainder is taken, so each face has exactly a 1 in 6 chance. A coin flip is a single random bit, so heads and tails are exactly 50/50.

Flip a coin or roll dice in 5 steps

  1. Open the Coin Flip & Dice Roller — it loads instantly and needs no account.
  2. Choose your tab. Coin flip for a yes/no decision, Dice roller when you need a number.
  3. For dice, pick how many to roll. From 1 to 6, starting at 2 — the classic pair for board games.
  4. Click Flip coin or Roll dice. The animation takes about a second, and the result is decided only when it ends — there is no way to stop it early on a favourable face.
  5. Read the result. The coin shows Heads or Tails and adds one to the tally; the dice show every face plus the total. Starting a new round? Click Reset tally first so old flips do not mix into the new count.

A worked example: 10 coin flips

Flip a coin 10 times and you should expect about 5 heads — but "about" does a lot of work there. Exactly 5 heads happens only 252 times out of 1,024 possible sequences, about 24.6%. All 10 heads is 1 in 1,024, about 0.098% — rare, but it happens to someone every day.

Streaks are just as ordinary: five heads in a row is 1 in 32 (3.125%), three in a row is 1 in 8 (12.5%). So a short run can easily read 7–3 with nothing wrong with the coin. Over 100 flips the tally should sit near 50 heads; after 10 flips it is mostly noise. For anything important, flip best-of-three or best-of-five.

A worked example: two dice and the number 7

Two dice have 36 possible combinations (6 × 6), but the totals are not equally likely. The total 7 has six combinations — 1+6, 2+5, 3+4, 4+3, 5+2, 6+1 — so it comes up 6 times out of 36, about 16.67%. Totals 2 and 12 have one combination each (1+1 and 6+6), about 2.78% each. A roll of 4+3 = 7 is the single most likely outcome.

Totals 2 to 12 come up 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 ways out of 36 — that is 2.78%, 5.56%, 8.33%, 11.11%, 13.89%, 16.67%, 13.89%, 11.11%, 8.33%, 5.56% and 2.78%.

Two consequences follow. Doubles happen 6 times out of 36, about 16.67%, and so does a total of 10 or more. The average total is exactly 7, because each die averages 3.5. With three dice the range widens to 3–18 across 216 combinations, and all three matching is 6 in 216, about 2.78%.

Coin flip vs dice roller vs random number generator

The three tools overlap, but each fits a different job. A coin flip is for a clean either/or: who goes first, yes or no. The dice roller is for games and totals. When you need a number outside the 1-to-6 range — a winner from 1 to 50, or 10 unique numbers — the Random Number Generator is the right tool: any minimum and maximum, up to 1,000 numbers at once, and it can force them all unique. Coin for decisions, dice for games, number generator for draws.

5 mistakes that make your coin flip or dice roll misleading

  • Expecting a short run to look 50/50. Ten flips landing 7–3 is normal. Judge fairness over 100 flips, not 10 — use the tally, not your memory of the last three results.
  • Thinking a result is "due" after a streak. Five heads in a row does not make tails more likely next — every flip is independent, with the same 50/50 chance. The coin has no memory.
  • Treating all dice totals as equally likely. A total of 7 is six times more likely than a 2 or a 12, so the middle totals quietly dominate any game that treats every total the same.
  • Using a coin flip for a decision it cannot settle. A flip picks between two options fairly; it cannot tell you which is better. Use it to break a tie, not to avoid thinking through a real choice.
  • Forgetting to reset the tally. The count keeps running until you press Reset tally. Start each new round with a reset, or the old numbers will make the new round look skewed.

Frequently asked questions

Is the coin flip really 50/50?
Yes. Each flip is decided by your browser's cryptographically secure random generator, so heads and tails each have a 1 in 2 chance and every flip is independent of the last. Short runs will still swing away from 50/50 — normal randomness, not a biased coin.

How many dice can I roll at once?
From 1 to 6 dice. The default is 2. Choose the number in the dropdown, click Roll dice, and the tool shows every die face plus the total.

What is the most likely total with two dice?
7. Six of the 36 possible combinations add up to 7, about 16.67% of the time — six times more often than 2 or 12, which each have only one combination.

Does the tool save or upload my results?
No. With ToolsHub's tool everything runs in your browser, so your flips and rolls are never uploaded to a server or saved anywhere.
