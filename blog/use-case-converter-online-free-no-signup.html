How to Use a Case Converter Online

Fix text stuck in the wrong case in one click — UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case, Sentence case, camelCase and snake_case, free with no signup.

October 20, 2026 · 6 min read

You paste a heading into a document and it comes out SHOUTING IN ALL CAPS. Or you copy a title from a website and it arrives as "tHiS kInD oF mEsS". Or you need a variable name like monthlyReport2026 and you are retyping the same words with different capitalisation for the third time. Fixing letter case by hand is slow, boring, and easy to get wrong — one missed letter and the whole heading looks sloppy.

A case converter does the job in one click: paste your text, pick the case you want, copy the result. This guide shows you how to use the free Case Converter, what each of its seven options does, and the mistakes to avoid.

What the tool does

The Case Converter takes any text you paste or type and rewrites it in a different letter case. It offers seven conversions, each with its own button:

  • UPPERCASE — every letter becomes a capital: "hello" becomes "HELLO".
  • lowercase — every letter becomes small: "HELLO" becomes "hello".
  • Title Case — the first letter of every word becomes a capital: "hello world" becomes "Hello World".
  • Sentence case — only the first letter of each sentence is capitalised, the rest is lowered: "HELLO WORLD. HOW ARE YOU?" becomes "Hello world. How are you?"
  • aLtErNaTiNg cAsE — capitals and small letters alternate, for playful text: "hello" becomes "HeLlO".
  • camelCase — words are joined with no spaces, the first word lowercased and each later word capitalised: "hello world" becomes "helloWorld". A standard style for variable names in code.
  • snake_case — words are joined with underscores, all lowered: "hello world" becomes "hello_world". A standard style for file names, database fields, and Python variable names.

Everything runs entirely in your browser: no uploads, no signup, no limit on conversions.

Convert text in 5 steps

  1. Open the Case Converter — it loads instantly and works on any device with a browser.
  2. Type or paste your text into the input box. It can be a single word, a heading, a paragraph, or a whole block of copied text.
  3. Click the case button you want — UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case, Sentence case, aLtErNaTiNg cAsE, camelCase or snake_case. The converted text appears in the result area at once.
  4. Click "Copy result" to copy the converted text to your clipboard. If you click a case button with an empty box, the tool reminds you to type or paste some text first.
  5. Paste it where you need it — your document, spreadsheet, code editor, or message. The result is not saved anywhere, so copy it before you close the tab.

Two details surprise people. First, Title Case capitalises every word, including small words like "The" — it does not apply title style rules that keep words like "the", "and", or "of" lowercase. Second, the alternating pattern counts letters only: spaces do not reset it, so "fox" comes out as "fOx" — the "f" is the 14th letter, an even position, so it stays lowercase.

Which case should you use?

  • UPPERCASE: short warnings, labels, and headings that need emphasis. Avoid for long text — paragraphs in capitals are hard to read and look like shouting.
  • lowercase: normalising copied text, email addresses, usernames, and URLs.
  • Title Case: headings, article titles, and names of things. Check small words afterwards if your style guide keeps them lowercase.
  • Sentence case: fixing pasted text that arrived in all caps, and ordinary sentences.
  • aLtErNaTiNg cAsE: jokes, memes, and playful messages only — never anything serious.
  • camelCase: variable and function names in JavaScript, Java, and similar languages.
  • snake_case: file names, database columns, and Python variables.

Rules worth knowing before you convert

Sentence case does more than lowercasing: it capitalises the first letter after a full stop, exclamation mark, question mark, or ellipsis. So "hello world. this is a test! is it working? yes, it is." becomes "Hello world. This is a test! Is it working? Yes, it is."

camelCase and snake_case both strip punctuation and spacing. "Hello, World! 2026 Report" becomes "helloWorld2026Report" and "hello_world_2026_report" — the comma and exclamation mark disappear, and numbers stay attached to the word they follow. Snake_case also removes accents, so "café au lait" becomes "cafe_au_lait". That is what you want for file names and code, but not for prose you want to keep punctuated.

5 mistakes that make case conversion go wrong

  • Trusting Title Case for publication titles. The tool capitalises every word, so "the" becomes "The". Most title styles keep small words lowercase — if the title will be published, fix them by hand.
  • Losing acronyms in Sentence case. Sentence case lowercases everything except sentence starts, so "NASA" becomes "Nasa". Re-check acronyms, initials, and proper names after converting.
  • Using camelCase or snake_case on normal prose. These options remove punctuation and spaces by design, and you cannot get the punctuation back from the result — keep the original until you are sure.
  • Using alternating case for anything that matters. It is a joke format, unreadable beyond a few words, and unprofessional in work messages, headings, or documents.
  • Closing the tab before copying. The result is not saved anywhere. Click "Copy result" and paste it into your document before you navigate away, or you will convert the text again.

Frequently asked questions

What is a case converter?
A case converter changes the capitalisation of text — from all capitals to sentence case, or from spaced words to camelCase or snake_case — without retyping. Paste the text, choose the case, copy the result.

Can I convert a whole paragraph at once?
Yes. Paste the whole block and click one button — the conversion applies to everything in the box. Sentence case is the most useful option for long pasted text, because it fixes every sentence at once.

Is my text uploaded or stored anywhere?
No. The Case Converter runs entirely in your browser. Your text never leaves your device, nothing is saved, and there is no account or signup.

Which case should I use for file names?
snake_case — "monthly_report_2026" — because spaces in file names cause problems in links and scripts, and underscores are safe everywhere. For code, follow your language's convention: camelCase in JavaScript and Java, snake_case in Python.
