A worked example: $29.99 at the default rate

Take a gadget listed at $29.99, at the default rate of 278 rupees per dollar:

  • $29.99 to PKR: 29.99 × 278 = 8,337.22 PKR. Check: 30 × 278 = 8,340, minus 0.01 × 278 = 2.78, so 8,340 − 2.78 = 8,337.22.
  • $49.99 to PKR: 49.99 × 278 = 13,897.22 PKR. Check: 50 × 278 = 13,900, minus 2.78 = 13,897.22.
  • $200 shopping budget to PKR: 200 × 278 = 55,600 PKR. A 55,600 rupee budget is exactly $200 at the default rate.
  • 10,000 PKR back to USD: the reverse direction divides: 10,000 ÷ 278 = 35.9712 USD. Check: 35.9712 × 278 = 9,999.99, which rounds to 10,000.

5 mistakes that make your USD to PKR conversion wrong

  • Trusting the default rate for a real payment. The default of 278 is a fixed starting point, not a live market rate. For actual money, enter your transaction's rate first, then read the result.
  • Multiplying when you should divide. Dollars to rupees multiplies by the per-USD rate; rupees to dollars divides by it. Converting 27,800 PKR back means 27,800 ÷ 278 = 100 USD — multiply instead and you get a nonsense 7.7 million.
  • Forgetting fees and spreads. Banks, cards and transfer services add a margin or fee, so the amount that actually lands can differ from the converted figure. A 3% fee on $100 is 834 PKR at the default rate.
  • Mixing up "per USD" with "per unit". All the tool's rates are units per 1 USD. A rate quoted the other way round — dollars per rupee, about 0.0036 — makes every answer wrong.
  • Rounding mid-calculation. Keep full precision and round only the final answer. Rounding the $29.99 result (8,337.22) to 8,337 before adding a fee, then rounding again, drifts the total a few rupees on every step.
